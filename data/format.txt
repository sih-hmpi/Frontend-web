1) Table design (rows = State + Metal)

Each row represents one (state, metal) measurement bucket. There are 24 columns (fits your 20–30 requirement) and they’re purposely chosen to support app features, analysis, policy dashboards, and research.

Columns (name → brief meaning)

1. state — State / UT name


2. region — Macro-region (e.g., Indo-Gangetic Plain, Deccan Plateau, NE India)


3. hydrogeo_zone — Hydrogeological zone name (if available)


4. metal — Metal name (As, Pb, Cr, Cd, Hg, Ni, Fe, Mn, Cu, Zn, U, Se, etc.)


5. unit — canonical unit (mg/L)


6. avg_conc — average concentration (mg/L) — latest period available


7. median_conc — median concentration (mg/L)


8. min_conc — minimum observed (mg/L)


9. max_conc — maximum observed (mg/L)


10. pct_sites_above_WHO — % sampled sites above WHO guideline (where WHO guideline exists)


11. pct_sites_above_BIS — % sampled sites above BIS / Indian standard (if defined)


12. n_samples — number of samples used to compute the stats (sample size)


13. years_sampled — year range of samples (e.g., 2018–2024)


14. detection_methods — common methods used (ICP-MS, AAS, ICP-OES, colorimetric)


15. depth_range_m — typical depth of sampled wells (m)


16. urban_pct — approximate % of samples from urban locations


17. rural_pct — approximate % of samples from rural locations


18. likely_sources — dominant contamination source(s) (geogenic / industrial / agricultural / mining)


19. affected_population_est — rough population estimate exposed (if available)


20. remediation_present — Yes/No/Partial (are mitigation measures in place?)


21. trend — Recent trend (increasing / stable / decreasing / patchy)


22. data_quality — Low/Medium/High (based on n_samples, recency, method)


23. gis_available — Yes/No (are geo-coordinates available for samples)


24. primary_references — short citations or URLs (CGWB, peer-reviewed papers, state bulletins)

5) Caveats & data quality notes (important for app/research use)

Heterogeneous sampling: CGWB data and academic studies differ in spatial coverage and sampling methodology. CGWB provides a national monitoring network (NHNS), but many peer-reviewed studies are local/targeted (industrial, mining, or known hotspot investigations). Combine both carefully.

Unit conversions: many papers use µg/L; I converted to mg/L for the table. Keep canonical units in your DB.

Temporal heterogeneity: some hotspot measurements are decades-old; use years_sampled to avoid mixing eras. For trend computations, always group by year windows (e.g., 5-yr).

Geolocation availability: CGWB CSVs often include station coordinates, but many published papers report only district-level info. For mapping, prefer CGWB station CSVs.

Regulatory thresholds: WHO guideline for As = 0.01 mg/L (10 µg/L), BIS may have different values. Some indices use different thresholds — record which standard you used when flagging exceedance.
