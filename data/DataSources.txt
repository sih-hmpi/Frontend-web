Perfect — now you’re thinking at the “India-scale rasterized groundwater observatory” level 🚀. To unify this into 3km × 3km raster grids (a nationwide geo-tiled dataset), you need to pull data from multiple institutional, governmental, and scientific sources, then harmonize them. Here’s a breakdown of where to get data + how to unify it:


---

🔹 1. Groundwater Quality (Heavy Metals, Pollutants)

Central Ground Water Board (CGWB)

Groundwater Year Books contain district-wise water quality data.

They run the National Aquifer Mapping (NAQUIM) program, which includes chemical quality data.

Metals: Arsenic, Fluoride, Nitrate, Iron, Lead, Chromium, etc.


National Water Quality Monitoring Program (NWMP) – CPCB

~2500 monitoring stations across rivers and groundwater.

Data on Zn, Cu, Ni, Pb, Cr, Cd, Hg, Fe, Mn, As, Se.

Accessible via ENVIS or CPCB datasets.


BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards)

Standards for drinking water quality (IS 10500). Use as thresholds.




---

🔹 2. Geological & Soil Sources (Heavy Metal Potential)

Geological Survey of India (GSI)

Baseline geochemistry maps → heavy metal distribution in rocks/soils.

Helps trace geogenic vs anthropogenic sources of metals.


National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC, ISRO)

Bhuvan Portal datasets → land use, soil properties, pollution mapping.


NBSS&LUP (ICAR)

Soil survey → trace elements, fertility, pH buffering (affects leaching).




---

🔹 3. Agricultural & Industrial Sources

Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare

Fertilizer/pesticide use data → Cd, As, Hg contamination risk.


Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)

Industrial hotspot mapping → tanneries (Cr), smelters (Pb, Zn), coal belts (Hg).


CPCB – Inventories of industrial clusters

“Critically Polluted Areas” list with water contamination indicators.




---

🔹 4. Public Health & Epidemiology

ICMR (Indian Council of Medical Research)

Health impacts (arsenicosis in Bengal, fluorosis in Rajasthan, etc.).


State Health Departments

Case registries linking groundwater with health burdens.




---

🔹 5. Historical Trends

India-WRIS (Water Resources Information System)

Historical groundwater levels and quality.


CGWB Time-Series Wells

Data available since the 1980s → arsenic, fluoride, nitrate.


Research Articles & Meta-Studies

Arsenic in Bengal delta (30 years), Fluoride in Rajasthan, Uranium in Punjab.




---

🔹 6. Remote Sensing & Global Datasets

NASA GRACE (groundwater depletion)

World Bank / UNICEF JMP (drinking water quality reports)

FAO Aquastat

IAEA GEMAS project (baseline metal levels).



---

🔹 7. Unification Pipeline (Rasterization)

1. Collect raw data → CGWB, CPCB, GSI, NRSC, ICMR, literature.


2. Geocode & normalize → convert district/block data to point/raster form.


3. Rasterize into 3×3 km tiles → use Google Earth Engine / PostGIS + QGIS.


4. Attributes per raster cell (30 columns example):

Location (lat, lon, state, district, block)

Depth (shallow, medium, deep aquifer)

pH, TDS, EC

Arsenic, Fluoride, Nitrate, Iron, Lead, Chromium, Cadmium, Mercury, Selenium, Uranium, Zinc, Copper, Nickel, Manganese

Source: Geogenic / Anthropogenic

Land Use Class (Agri/Industrial/Urban)

Population exposed

Health cases (fluorosis, arsenicosis)

Time-series (1980 → Present)

Risk Index



5. Final Integration → A nationwide geospatial database for app + research + policy input.




---

✅ By unifying all this, your app won’t just be a monitoring tool — it will become the first India-scale heavy metal groundwater risk atlas, usable by policy makers, researchers, NGOs, and governments.
